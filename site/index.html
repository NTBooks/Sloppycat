There's a song on your artist page and you didn't make it.

Here's how it gets there. Someone runs a track through an AI, signs up with a cheap distributor, and tells them it's yours. Nobody checks. It shows up on your profile next to your real records and the royalties go to them. Your fans think it's you. Same trick with books, except it's your name on a knockoff, or a title one word off yours. Sloppycat watches your profiles and tells you when something lands there that you didn't put there.

For artists and authors

You tell it what's yours, once.

No detector knows your catalog. You do. So it asks you instead, one time, and then it watches the page for you. Most weeks you won't hear from it, which is sort of the point.

Snapshot your profile

Open your own artist or author page and press the button. It reads what's on there the same way anyone else's browser would. Everything starts ticked as yours.

Untick what isn't yours

Anything odd gets a note about what looked off, usually a label you've never heard of on something that turned up last week. It's a reason to look. It isn't proof of anything.

Send the takedown

Everything you untick comes with the letter already written, the right form for that platform, your links and the release id filled in. You copy it and send it. That part still has to be you.

After that it checks on a timer, hourly unless you change it. Anything new you haven't claimed turns into an alert with the takedown attached. It also goes looking for near-copies of your titles sitting somewhere else under a made-up name, because watching your own page never catches those and that's half of what's going on.

If you just listen to music and read books

You get the fakes marked before you press play.

It works like the filter lists in your ad blocker. Subscribe to an artist's list, or to the community one, and their stuff gets a badge on the page. Hover it and you get who said what, and when they said it.

✓ the artist says it's theirs
✗ the artist says it isn't
◷ older than the slop wave, probably fine
○ enrolled artist, not yet confirmed
No badge means no list covers it yet. You get nothing instead of a guess, which I think is the right way round. And if something is yours and you did use AI on part of it, you can say which part and it shows up on the card, instead of people arguing about it in your comments.

The lists

Your list is a markdown file.

That's the whole format. One file, put anywhere that gives you a URL, and a gist takes about a minute. Anyone can read it without installing anything, and if it's wrong they can open a pull request instead of emailing me and waiting a week. 

# Jane Doe verified catalog
Title: Jane Doe verified catalog
Type: creator

## Creator
| platform | profile |
| spotify  | https://open.spotify.com/artist/0123456789abcdefghijkl |

## Mine
| platform | id                     | title     | disclosure                     |
| spotify  | 4aBcdefghijklmnopqrstu | Blue Room | vocals:human; art:ai-generated |

## Not mine
| platform | id                     | title               | first seen | note                 |
| spotify  | 9xYcdefghijklmnopqrstu | Midnight Jazz Vibes | 2026-09-14 | not ours, reported   |
Proving it's you doesn't need an account. You paste the list URL into a bio only you can edit, in Spotify for Artists or Author Central, and the list names that profile back. Both directions have to match, and the second half is the one that matters: anyone can write a list claiming your catalog, but they can't make your profile point at it. Everyone's copy checks that for itself before it shows a badge.

Install

It isn't in the store yet.

For now you build it and load the folder yourself. Chrome, developer mode, load unpacked, point it at dist. Firefox once the Chrome one stops moving around. 

npm install
npm run build
Get it on GitHub Back to the top
Worth saying up front: checks only run while your browser is open, because that's as far as an extension reaches. Leave Chrome running and hourly means hourly. Close it Friday night and it picks up on Monday.

This is a band-aid

The actual fix is the platform asking you first, before it puts a release on your page. Spotify is testing that now, which is good, and it took a couple of years of this happening first. Amazon and Goodreads have nothing like it. So until they get around to it, this is the part you can do yourself without waiting for anyone's permission.

tl;dr: people upload AI slop under other people's names and the platforms wave it through. You snapshot your own profile, untick what isn't yours, and it watches the page and writes your takedown letters. Everyone else can subscribe to your list and see the junk flagged before they press play. It doesn't fix the hole. It does mean you find out in an hour instead of whenever a fan gets round to emailing you.
