# The problem

Researched 2026-09-22.

## Music: the release is attached to a real artist's profile

The mechanics, as reported consistently across outlets: someone generates a track with a tool like Suno or
Udio, generates cover art, signs up with a cheap distributor such as DistroKid, and claims to be an existing
artist. The distributor doesn't verify. The release is delivered to the platforms mapped to that artist's
existing profile, so it appears on their page alongside real records, and the royalties go to the uploader.

- 404 Media demonstrated the hole by publishing an unauthorized track onto the profile of the band Lathe of
  Heaven.
- Jazz musicians have been hit repeatedly. Jason Moran, Carsten Dahl, Thomas Blachman and Chris Minh Doky
  are named in the coverage. One artist reported Spotify removing the material about 72 hours after contact.
- The group Odette Child reported finding more than 300 AI tracks posing as existing acts.
- Scale, for context: Deezer reported AI-generated tracks at 44% of daily uploads in April 2026, passing 50%
  by July, and said in January 2026 that 85% of streams of AI tracks looked fraudulent.

## Books: two different attacks

**Under the author's name.** Jane Friedman found six books on Amazon and Goodreads carrying her name that she
didn't write. Goodreads removed them quickly. Amazon initially refused, on the grounds that she hadn't
trademarked her name, and relented after the story spread.

**Cloning a title that sold.** Knockoffs of a new release appear with near-identical titles and covers under
invented author names, sometimes blending parts of the real author's name. Canadian author Jessica Waite's
*A Widow's Guide to Dead Bastards* is a documented case.

The Authors Guild has been tracking this as a category since 2023 and runs a scam-alert page.

## Why existing AI labelling doesn't address it

Spotify began applying an "AI Persona" badge to artist accounts identified as non-human in September 2026, and
Deezer tags AI-generated tracks for listeners. Spotify also carries AI disclosure through DDEX credits, which
the uploader fills in.

None of that catches a hijack. The fake sits on a human artist's profile and is credited to that human, so no
AI badge applies to it, and a scammer impersonating someone is not going to volunteer a disclosure. Detection
answers "was this made with AI", and the question that matters here is "did this artist release it". Only the
artist can answer the second one.

## Prior art

- **Spotify Artist Profile Protection** (beta since March 2026): eligible artists get an email when a release
  is delivered under their name and can approve or decline it, plus an "artist key" they can hand to trusted
  providers. This is the actual fix. It was still described as a limited beta as of the last coverage, with no
  published eligibility criteria, and it covers Spotify only.
- **Too Lost "Profile Defender"**: monitoring plus takedowns, offered by a distributor to its own clients.
- **AI Music Badge** and similar extensions: flag AI-generated artists, which is the detection question, not
  the ownership one.
- Nothing equivalent exists for books on any platform.
